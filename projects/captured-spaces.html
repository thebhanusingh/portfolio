Captured Spaces

Real spaces captured on a phone and turned into Gaussian splats you can move around in, right in the browser: an office interior and a backyard exterior, both from my own capture-to-splat pipeline.

Gaussian SplattingnerfstudioCOLMAPhlocPlayCanvasOpen source

Office interior

A handheld 4K walk-through of an office, about three minutes long, with tilts up to the ceiling. Interiors are the hard case for capture: big blank white walls and ceilings give the camera solver nothing to match. COLMAP could place only 72% of 1,000 frames, even after doubling the frame density. Switching to hloc — learned SuperPoint features matched with LightGlue, with NetVLAD retrieval to choose image pairs — placed 981 of the 1,000 frames (98%) in a single model. I then trained the splat at full 1080p resolution on my 8 GB laptop GPU (splatfacto-big, 30,000 steps, about 50 minutes).

Backyard exterior

A 1080p, 60 fps phone video of a backyard: house siding, sheds, a fence and dense planting. Trained on the school workstation (RTX PRO 6000, 96 GB) with splatfacto-big for 60,000 steps, it grew to 4.15 million Gaussians, almost three times what my laptop could fit. For the web it's reduced to 2.3 million Gaussians with base color only, at 27 MB.

The process

One command takes a video, a scene name, a frame count and a step count, runs every stage below, and stops the moment any stage fails, so one bad run can't roll into the next.

  1. 01 · Capture

    A slow handheld phone video, walking around the space. Keep furniture or an edge in frame: every stretch of bare wall or ceiling is a stretch the solver can't place.

  2. 02 · Frame selection

    Split the video into 650 to 1,000 windows and keep the sharpest frame in each, scored by the variance of the Laplacian, instead of evenly spaced frames. Motion blur was the main cause of soft detail.

  3. 03 · Camera solve

    COLMAP structure-from-motion. For low-texture interiors, hloc instead: SuperPoint features matched with LightGlue, NetVLAD retrieval to choose pairs, and pycolmap to build the model.

  4. 04 · Pose repair

    My own step: keep the largest sub-model, bundle-adjust it with principal-point refinement, and keep COLMAP's world coordinates so the splat lines up with the tracking data an Unreal or LED-stage scene will use.

  5. 05 · Training

    nerfstudio splatfacto-big at full resolution with camera-pose refinement. Point growth is capped so it fits an 8 GB GPU; the interior also uses antialiased rendering and scale regularization for its blank walls.

  6. 06 · Clean-up and web

    Crop to the 1st–99th percentile box of opaque splats plus 10%, run a floater filter, rotate upright, and compress to PlayCanvas SOG: about 320 MB down to 19 MB for the office.

Test criteria

The bigger risk in this pipeline turned out to be silent failure, not crashes: standard tools can print a warning and keep going. These checks run on every scene, and each one exists because it caught a real problem.

Pose coverage

Pass: at least 80% of frames posed in one model, or the pipeline stops before training.
Caught: the office solved only 72% with COLMAP. Switching to hloc reached 98%.

Frame count

Pass: the frames with camera poses match the frames extracted.
Caught: a run that was about to train on 2 of 659 frames after COLMAP split the scene.

Alignment

Pass: splat points sit on the camera-tracking points.
Measured: median distance of 0.058 units in a scene about 5–7 units across.

Held-out accuracy

Pass: render frames the model never trained on, apply the same lens correction and a small alignment step, then compare with PSNR and zoomed crops. Never PSNR alone.
Measured: 21.2 and 22.5 dB medians on two outdoor scenes. The sharper workstation splat scored lower until the alignment step, because PSNR rewards blur.

Resources

Pass: GPU and system memory trends stay below set limits (an alert at 7.2 of 8 GB GPU), with crash and slowdown alerts while training runs.
Caught: the large model heading for the 8 GB ceiling, and a run that would have exhausted system memory.

Reproducibility

Pass: the setup rebuilds on a second machine from the notes alone.
Caught: a COLMAP update had renamed an option nerfstudio still used. Versions are now pinned.

Web delivery

Pass: under 30 MB, reads back as a valid SOG with the expected splat count, and renders upright.
Result: 19 MB office (1.24M splats), 27 MB backyard (2.3M splats).

Built on open source

I built this pipeline by connecting open-source tools, with Claude Code as my coding agent, and wrote the glue myself: the sharp-frame selector, the pose-repair step, the end-to-end run script, and the checks above. The method is 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al., 2023); training uses nerfstudio's own Apache-licensed implementation, not the original research code.
